Almost every adult experiences low back pain at least once in their life — whether due to prolonged sitting, an awkward movement, or gradual buildup of muscle tension. At Dr. Verbitskiy's practice in Maribor we treat many patients each year, from mild occasional pain to long-standing chronic conditions that interfere with daily life.

Causes

Why low back pain occurs

Pain in the lumbar spine rarely stems from a single cause — it is usually a combination of prolonged strain and a triggering event. The most common causes we observe in patients are:

  • 🔹 Prolonged sitting at a computer at work or while driving
  • 🔹 Incorrect lifting technique or sudden jerking movements
  • 🔹 Weak deep abdominal and back muscles that do not adequately support the spine
  • 🔹 Degenerative changes of the intervertebral discs due to aging
  • 🔹 Excess body weight that further loads the lumbar spine
  • 🔹 Prolonged stress manifesting as chronic muscle tension in the low back
Symptoms

How low back pain presents

Low back pain can present in different ways depending on the cause and duration. Recognizing the pain pattern helps us choose the right treatment.

  • 🔹 Dull, constant pain that worsens after prolonged sitting or standing
  • 🔹 Sharp pain when bending, twisting or standing up
  • 🔹 Morning stiffness that gradually improves during the day
  • 🔹 Feeling of a muscle cramp or 'block' in the lumbar region
  • 🔹 Pain that spreads toward the buttock but does not radiate into the leg
  • 🔹 Worsening of pain when coughing or sneezing
How we help

How we treat low back pain

Treatment with Dr. Verbitskiy always begins with a precise assessment, since the same pain can arise from entirely different causes. Based on the findings we select a combination of manual techniques that best suit your condition.

1. Assessment and diagnosis

Discussion of pain onset, clinical examination of posture and lumbar mobility.

2. Manual therapy

Targeted manual techniques to release muscle tension and restrictions in the lumbar area.

3. Recommendations for daily life

Advice on posture, movement and prevention of pain recurrence.

When to seek medical help

Signs that require immediate attention

In rare cases low back pain may be a sign of a more serious condition that requires immediate medical assessment. Seek medical help right away if you notice sudden severe pain after an injury, loss of bladder or bowel control, fever, unexplained weight loss, or progressive weakness or numbness/tingling in both legs. In such cases manual therapy is not the appropriate first step — urgent medical evaluation is required.

Frequently asked questions

What commonly causes lower back pain?

Common causes include muscle strains, repetitive loading, poor posture and degenerative changes of the spine. Less common causes include inflammatory conditions or structural issues that may require further assessment.

When should I seek urgent medical attention?

Seek urgent care if pain follows an injury, is accompanied by leg weakness or numbness, there are problems with bladder or bowel control, or if you have fever—these situations need prompt evaluation.

What services does spine practice in Maribor provide for back pain?

We provide clinical assessment, individual exercise therapy, individualized exercise programs, ergonomic and lifestyle advice, and coordinate imaging or specialist referrals when appropriate.

How can I help relieve lower back pain at home?

Maintain gentle activity as tolerated, perform recommended mobility and strengthening exercises, adjust workplace ergonomics, and use heat or cold for short-term relief. If symptoms persist or worsen, consult a healthcare professional.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions about back pain

How long does it take for back pain to go away?

Mild, acute pain often subsides within a few days to two weeks, while chronic problems may require several weeks of targeted treatment.

Should I rest when I have back pain?

Complete rest for more than a day or two is usually not recommended — cautious movement and targeted treatment often speed recovery more than total rest.

Is back pain always a sign of a herniated disc?

No, most back pain comes from muscles, joints, or ligaments and is not related to a herniated disc. A thorough assessment during examination will reveal the likely cause.

Can I help myself with exercises at home?

Gentle stretching and stabilization exercises can help, but we recommend aligning them with our guidance after assessment, as incorrect exercises may worsen the condition.

When should I book an appointment?

If the pain lasts more than a few days, recurs, or limits daily activities, it is sensible to have an exam as soon as possible.

Is manual therapy painful?

Techniques are tailored to your tolerance and condition — the goal is relaxation, not additional pain.
